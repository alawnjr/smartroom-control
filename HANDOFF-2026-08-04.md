## DO THIS FIRST — the one thing that is written but not switched on

**Run `reolink_av_forward.py` in place of the two old forwarders**, on the laptop
that can reach the NVR:

```bash
# stop the two separate forwarders, then
python reolink_av_forward.py          # audio + video, one RTSP session
```

Why it matters: `reolink_live_forward.py` and `reolink_audio_forward.py` each open
their **own** RTSP session to the same camera and drop the other's stream. The
camera hands audio and video over on one timebase; two processes throw that away,
and the server then stamps each on arrival — so the only thing relating a sound to a
picture is how long each pipeline took. Those pipelines are not comparable (one
decodes h264 and re-encodes mjpeg, the other only encodes mp3). **That difference
is what `SMARTROOM_AUDIO_TRIM_MS=2790` is measuring.** It is not a property of the
camera, it is a property of our own plumbing, and it moves with load.

The replacement splits inside ONE ffmpeg, so both outputs carry positions on one
input clock. Measured against a synthetic flash+beep source
(`CityOSNode/test/av_split_probe.py`): **0.000 s of A/V skew introduced, no drift**,
versus 2.79 s of hand correction. What remains is ~90 ms of constant libmp3lame
priming.

**How to tell it worked** — `curl http://172.16.60.239:8010/audio/status`:

```
measured: true,  mediaClock: {"points": <non-zero>}     ← good
measured: false, mediaClock: {"points": 0}              ← still the OLD forwarder
measured: true,  mediaClock: {"points": 0}              ← STOP: audio is being
                                                          placed against nothing
```

Two things to check on first contact with the real NVR, since none of this has met
it: that the **sub** stream actually carries audio (if the NVR only puts audio on
the main stream, the audio-bearing channel needs `--stream main`), and that the
`?media=1` framing survives the connection. Once `measured: true`,
`SMARTROOM_AUDIO_TRIM_MS` is dead config.

## Recorded audio

The record button records room audio **into the clip**, not beside it. While a
segment is open the recorder taps the relay, writes `.{cam}_audio.mp3`, and muxes it
into the mp4 by stream copy at close. Only ch1's clip carries it — one microphone;
five copies would echo in the synced player. `metadata.json` gains an `audio` block
only where a track really exists, with `skew_ms` and `alignment`.

Four separate defects had to be fixed before a take had usable sound, and each hid
the next:

1. **Mute takes.** ch1 is the only microphone AND the camera that localizes nobody
   (2.6 m up, its floor-ray overshoots), so the people test discarded its segment
   every time and the sound went with it. The audio-bearing clip is now exempt.
2. **Truncated sound.** Clips are encoded at a blind CFR 30 while the cameras
   deliver ~10 fps, so a 25 s take became an 8 s container and `-shortest` cut 26 s
   of audio to 8.2 s. The audio-bearing clip is now retimed (`-itsscale 30/real_fps`).
3. **Silent openings.** Audio is dated later than it arrives, so at the instant
   Record is pressed the sound covering the take's first seconds has already gone
   past. `AudioRelay.backlog` replays it into a starting segment.
4. **The correction never reached the file.** `-itsoffset` with a **negative** value
   asks for negative timestamps, which mp4 cannot express — ffmpeg clamps to zero
   and drops the shift **with no error**. Since the backlog makes every skew
   negative, every clip carried `skew_ms` in its metadata and none of it in the
   container. Negative skew now trims the audio HEAD (`-ss`) instead.

Verified by putting a burst in the sound and measuring where it lands: predicted
1.21 s, measured **1.210 s** (unshifted it sits at 4.0 s). Duration alone cannot
show this — `-shortest` caps the audio at the video's length either way, which is
why the first version of that test was worthless.

## Recording behaviour

- **One press of Record is now one clip.** Segments were cut on a 3-minute
  wall-clock grid, which belongs to the always-on mode — off by default, and never
  what the grid was conditioned on. A take started at 11:44:58 came out as a
  2-second stub plus a separate recording. `RECORD.segment()` now decides: the
  take's own start when someone started it, the wall clock only for always-on,
  `TAKE_MAX_S` (30 min) a ceiling rather than a rhythm. `SMARTROOM_SEGMENT_S` is
  dead config unless always-on returns.
- **Every camera keeps its clip** (`SMARTROOM_SEGMENT_MIN_FRAMES=0`). The old 15
  threw a clip away unless 15 frames held a *localized* person, which made whole
  cameras vanish from a take and left empty-room takes with no folder at all.
  Pressing Record is already the decision that the footage is wanted. Costs GPU
  time in the analysis pass and rows in the listing, not meaningful disk
  (~10 MB/min for all five cameras).
- **Spatial estimation is depth-only, now enforced offline too.**
  `SMARTROOM_SPATIAL_ONLY=camera_d455_color` was set on the live service but not on
  `smartroom-analyze.service`, so `localize.py` would have floor-rayed Reolink clips
  the moment one appeared without a live-written sidecar. Reolink footage was clean
  only by an ordering accident. Now stated on both.

## The mirror (smartroom-mirror)

**Recorded playback is actually synchronised.** It aligned cameras on
`hw_timestamp_ms`, which is not one clock — librealsense capture time for the D455,
the forwarding host's wall clock for the Reolinks, ~1.6 s apart for the same
instant — and then zeroed each clip against whichever CSV loaded first. Five tiles,
five timelines, and a transport reporting 1.0 s for a 20 s take. `sync_ms` (server
arrival less each camera's measured delay) was already in every CSV and read by
nothing. On `rec_20260804_120357` the five cameras now start within **75 ms** of
each other and the session is 19.97 s.

**Recorded playback has sound.** It was silent by construction: `CanvasDriver`
decodes through WebCodecs onto a canvas so its `<video>` never plays, and
`SeekDriver` mutes its `<video>`. The track now rides its own `<audio>` element on
the same master clock (`lib/audio-sync.ts`), tracking to within 50 ms measured in a
live browser. A speaker button appears only for recordings that have a track.

**Light theme, white as the page colour.** Every dark literal moved to a variable;
the accent drops to a blue that passes contrast on white; the 3D room is themed
in JS because WebGL cannot see CSS variables, and its grid has to *darken* on white
or it vanishes. A boot script stamps `data-theme` before first paint so neither
theme flashes the other.

**Card dashboard layout**, per the reference design: four columns (controls,
readouts, room, camera tiles) with the filmstrip along the bottom. The readout cards
state what the room contains, driven by the scene's own fused people (published on
change, not per frame). No confidence percentage — the pipeline does not carry a
per-person action confidence into the fused view, and inventing one seemed worse
than omitting it.

**Two layout bugs that only appeared on a 1440 px MacBook**, both mine, neither
browser-specific:

- A media query adds **no specificity**, so `.info { display: flex }` sitting below
  `@media (max-width: 1500px) { .info { display: none } }` beat it at every width.
  The readout column stayed visible with no grid column to live in and was
  auto-flowed into a row of its own under the filmstrip.
- The shell is `width: 100%` with 12 px padding and there was **no `box-sizing`
  reset**, so it measured **1464 px in a 1440 px viewport** and clipped its own
  right-hand column.

Placement is now by grid **line** rather than area name (a line cannot fail to
resolve, and an item that fails to place does not fail quietly — it moves
elsewhere), responsive rules live last in the file, and three tests pin all of it.
Verified by driving headless Chrome over the DevTools protocol at 1280/1440/1728.

**The 3D floor marker sits under its skeleton.** The bones came from one estimate of
where a person is and the ring, box and label from another — the trunk-range
centroid — so the marker stood a stride away. Both branches of `resolvePose` now
derive it from the joints (`groundOf`: ankles, else hips, else the visible-joint
mean; a sitting person behind a desk has no ankles). Measured on real D455
calibration, the old code put the marker **1.39 m** from the skeleton's own ankles.
This also fixes cross-camera merging, which paired people on that same value.

## Traps worth knowing (additions to HANDOFF.md's list)

- **`-r` is not a clock; the `fps` filter is.** `-r 10` on an 11 s source emitted
  **112** frames where 11 × 10 predicts 110, so `frame_index / fps` mislabelled
  every frame by 0.2 s. A bare JPEG stream has nowhere to put a timestamp, so the
  index *is* the clock and it has to be honest.
- **`-itsoffset` only shifts one way.** Negative values are silently dropped by mp4.
- **Duration cannot prove an audio shift happened** — `-shortest` hides it. Put a
  marker in the sound and measure where it lands.
- **Two clocks that look independent may be one clock plus a constant we created.**
  Worth asking of any calibration constant here whether the thing being measured was
  manufactured upstream by us.
- **A media query cannot outrank a later rule.** Keep responsive blocks last.
- **A test that passes may be passing on nothing.** My first version of the A/V
  probe used an invented camera matrix; `liftSkeleton` returned `null` and every
  assertion sailed through. Assert that the subject exists before asserting about it.

## Corrections to things I asserted during this work

- I claimed byte-based mp3 timing drifts ~0.3 s over 30 s and used that to justify
  frame counting. **Measured at 6/12/30/60/120 s, both estimates are constant
  (+84…104 ms) and neither drifts.** Frame counting is still right — a chunk can
  only be tagged if it ends on a frame boundary, and the padding bit means byte
  arithmetic cannot find them — but not for the reason given.
- I first "fixed" the floor marker in the branch that uses `anchor.joints`, which no
  sidecar in this archive has, so the branch that actually renders was untouched and
  the bug survived a deploy.
